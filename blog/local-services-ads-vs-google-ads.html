Local Services Ads vs Google Ads: which one a local business should run

Kamran Arshad30 Oct 20265 min read

Local Services Ads charge when a customer contacts you. Google Ads charge when someone clicks. Local Services Ads are simpler and focused on people ready to call, while Google Ads give you far more control over searches, areas and landing pages.

For most eligible service businesses the useful question isn't which one, but which one to start with.

I've set up the account and I run Local Services Ads for a UK carpet cleaning company, alongside its Google Ads. The comparison below uses Google's own documentation for the facts and that hands-on work for the practical view.

The differences that matter

When to start with Local Services Ads

They suit a business in an eligible category that wants phone calls and messages with little campaign management. They also work without a website, although I'd still want one: a customer who sees your ad will often check you out before calling.

Eligibility is the first thing to check. Google limits these ads to certain service categories, and some features vary by country. Its help pages list categories such as plumbers, electricians and dentists, so look up your own category and country before planning around them.

When to start with Google Ads

Google Ads make more sense when you want to choose exactly which jobs you advertise, for example implants instead of check-ups, or boiler installs instead of small repairs. You also choose the landing page, which matters for high-value work where people compare before they call.

The price of that control is management. Search terms need reviewing, negative keywords need adding and conversions need tracking. Without that upkeep, Google Ads can spend your budget on clicks that never become enquiries.

Why many businesses run both

The two aren't either-or. Google itself suggests combining them to increase visibility. A sensible order for many local businesses is this:

  1. Fix your Business ProfileLocal Services Ads draw on your business information, and a complete profile helps your Maps visibility too.
  2. Start with whichever fits your goalChoose Local Services Ads for fast, simple leads, or Google Ads if you need to target specific jobs.
  3. Track every lead by sourceOtherwise you can't compare the two fairly.
  4. Add the second channelDo this once the first is producing leads you can measure.

The change coming to Local Services Ads

Moving into Google Ads

Google is moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals. The first phase began in August 2026 for select US advertisers, and accounts outside the US follow in 2027. Google says you still pay per valid lead and ads stay on Search and Maps, but past performance reports won't carry over, so save them before migrating.

What I'm not claiming

I'm not publishing lead costs or a verdict on which channel wins. Results vary by category, area and season, and one account isn't a benchmark. The honest way to decide is to run them side by side with tracking, then compare the cost of a booked job.

Common questions

Are Local Services Ads cheaper than Google Ads?

Not automatically. They bill per lead instead of per click, so the two prices aren't directly comparable. Compare the cost per booked job from each channel in your own area.

Do I need a website for Local Services Ads?

No. Google says leads can be managed directly without a website. A website still helps, because many customers check a business before calling.

Can I run Local Services Ads and Google Ads together?

Yes. Google suggests combining them to increase visibility.

Are Local Services Ads available in my country?

It depends on your country and category. Google limits them to certain service categories and some features vary by region, so check Google's help pages for your location.
