Google Ads for dentists: fill the chair with the treatments you want more of

Kamran ArshadUpdated 30 Oct 2026
United KingdomUnited StatesCanadaAustralia

A dental practice doesn't need more clicks. It needs more enquiries for the treatments that pay, from people close enough to come in, and a way to see which searches produced them.

I rebuild and run Google Ads accounts, and I have done it for a dental clinic: ad groups by treatment, with form submissions and phone calls tracked as conversions.

What I would set up for your practice

01

Campaigns split by treatment

Someone searching for implants and someone searching for a check-up are different patients with very different value. Implants, whitening, cleaning and emergency searches each get their own ad groups, ads and budget decisions.

02

Location targeting set to your catchment

Your ads only show where patients can realistically reach you. This sounds basic, but it is one of the first things I check, because the wrong default setting can spend budget far outside your area.

03

A negative keyword list

Dental searches attract people looking for jobs, courses, free treatment and information. A maintained negative list stops those clicks before you pay for them.

04

Calls and forms counted as conversions

Enquiries usually arrive by phone or form. Both are tracked through Google Tag Manager and imported into Google Ads, so bidding works towards patient enquiries and not just visits.

I completed advertiser verification, rebuilt the account as Search only, split ad groups by service, set local targeting, built the negative list and added tracking. For the first time, the clinic could see which ads and keywords were producing patient enquiries.

The fundamentals don't change with the country. Clicks and leads cost more in the UK and the US, which makes wasted spend more expensive and clean tracking more valuable. A dental account leaks money in the same places wherever it runs: broad targeting, irrelevant searches and enquiries nobody is counting.

What a dental account should be able to answer

  1. Which treatments bring enquiries?Spend and enquiries per treatment, so budget can follow the work you want.
  2. Which searches brought them?The actual search terms behind each enquiry, reviewed and cleaned regularly.
  3. What does an enquiry cost?Measured from your own tracked calls and forms, not from an industry average.
  4. Which enquiries became patients?If your practice records this, it can be fed back to Google Ads so bidding learns from real bookings.
