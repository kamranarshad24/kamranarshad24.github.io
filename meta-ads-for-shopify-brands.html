Meta ads for Shopify brands: scale on the orders you can actually count

Kamran ArshadUpdated 30 Oct 2026
United KingdomUnited StatesCanadaAustralia

Before a Shopify brand spends more on Meta, it needs to know whether the numbers in Ads Manager match the orders in Shopify. Often they don't, and bigger budgets then flow to the wrong campaigns.

I have run Facebook and Instagram ads for Shopify stores, and I start by checking reported purchases against real orders, so scaling decisions rest on numbers you can trust.

Where I start with a Shopify brand

01

Reconcile Meta against Shopify

I compare reported purchases with real orders. A gap isn't always a fault, because attribution windows differ, but you need to know how big it is and why.

02

Clean up the pixel and Conversions API

Duplicate pixels, app-installed tags and events sent twice all distort what Meta optimises towards. These get fixed before any budget moves.

03

Test products and creative on real margin

The best-looking ROAS can come from a product that loses money after costs and returns. I judge tests on the orders that actually get paid, not on what the platform reports about itself.

04

Then scale what holds up

Budget moves to campaigns that still work once the data is clean. You get a short update every week.

The fundamentals don't change with the country. Clicks and leads cost more in the UK and the US, which makes wasted spend more expensive and clean tracking more valuable. The same is true of e-commerce: the ad platform, the pixel and the store work the same way, and reported numbers need checking everywhere.

Common questions

Why doesn't Meta match my Shopify orders?

Some difference is normal, because Meta counts by its attribution window and Shopify counts orders when they happen. A large gap often points to a duplicate pixel, an app adding its own tracking or purchase events firing twice.

Do you work with stores on any platform?

The e-commerce work in my case studies is on Shopify, so that is where I can help most directly.

Do you run Google Shopping as well?

My e-commerce ad experience is on Meta and Snapchat, so that is what I offer. I run Google Ads for lead generation businesses, and I'd rather be clear about that than overclaim.
